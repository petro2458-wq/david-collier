About

My story

I'm a petroleum engineer. I spent 23 years in oil and gas, 15 at Hess and 8 at Kosmos Energy, working the Permian Basin, South Louisiana and the Gulf of Mexico, Equatorial Guinea, Ghana, and Norway. I started as a reservoir and production engineer doing hands-on field work and rose to Vice President, Global Production. About 70% of my career was on operated properties. The other 30% was non-operated, in unusual situations where the asset was critical to the company's success, such as Valhall for Hess and Jubilee for Kosmos.

At the end of 2019 I left Kosmos on my own terms to trade futures full-time. After many years of intense travel, it also let me be present during my kids' most formative years. I worked at trading seriously for six years. I had strong periods, including 2025 and early 2026, but the results were not consistent enough to build a long-term career on.

Now, as my kids get older, I'm looking for a long-term commitment to further my oil and gas career, in the field where my experience is deepest.

The time away added two things I bring with me: disciplined risk management under uncertainty, and real hands-on skill building working software with AI tools. Working for myself, I put far more time into AI than most full-time employees could, and I'm looking forward to applying it to engineering work.

Selected work

Key accomplishments

Production and operations

Started up 30,000+ bopd deepwater wells

I led production and operations on the Ceiba Field and the Okume Complex, personally starting up high-PI wells with more than 30,000 bopd of productivity. I built deep experience bringing wells with different gas volume fractions, water cuts, and reservoir pressures into a combined manifold, managing well and facility performance daily with the FPSO's offshore installation manager, and allocating water injection to maintain production.

Led a multi-year subsea pump campaign at Ceiba

Ceiba produced 40,000 bopd from 30 deepwater subsea wells, and its eleven subsea multiphase pumps were failing early. I led a multi-year root-cause and sparing strategy with FRAMO in Bergen, managing pump retrievals, teardowns, and design improvements, and the field later added gas lift. I also built a deferred-oil tracker and remote monitoring, improved subsea flow-meter calibration, and led a 20-person, 30-day vessel campaign for the baseline subsea inspection. It gave me a deep, hands-on understanding of subsea flow, artificial lift, and production engineering.

Kept Jubilee producing at 100,000 bopd

As Jubilee Unit Asset Manager, I set budgets and influenced the drilling program on Kosmos's only producing asset, and owned the gas-handling solution when re-injection became a reservoir constraint before gas export was ready.

Kept Jubilee running through a turret failure

We learn the most when things go wrong. When the Jubilee FPSO's turret failed, the field ran for more than three years with the vessel held on heading by tugs. I worked on the field operations plan for that period, the offloading strategy, the repair work running in parallel, and the insurance recovery.

Production engineering in South Louisiana

I ran production engineering for 100 onshore and offshore wells producing 52 MMcfed to 14 facilities: daily monitoring and optimization, gas lift optimization, data acquisition (cased-hole logs, production logs, pressure surveys), and workover prioritization and supervision to keep the field profitable. My workover program added 5 MMcfed over the 2002 decline for $1.5MM.

Served on Hess's production excellence team

I was one of eight managers worldwide on Hess's production excellence leadership team, focused on integrity, reliability, EHS and social responsibility, cost management, and production optimization.

Subsurface and development

Developed the Okume Complex to first oil

I led a subsurface team of 22 to identify drilling locations and develop the field with 2 rigs, to first oil. Phase I and II covered 32 wells and $1B of capital, with two tension leg platforms and three satellite platforms, and the field ramped to a peak of 80,000 bopd. I managed the interfaces between development, drilling and completions, subsurface, and operations, and we built multiple geologic model realizations for ten major reservoirs to produce low, best, and high forecasts.

Helped sanction and deliver TEN

I was integral to sanctioning the TEN plan of development in Ghana, which reached first oil in mid-2016. We built a multi-case development plan that captured the full range of resource uncertainty: third-party reserves work, economic optimization of capital, and our own subsurface geologic models and field development plans, so we could properly influence the operator.

Built full field development plans

I created full field development plans for large fields in Equatorial Guinea and Ghana, coordinating geoscience and engineering teams and integrating drilling, facilities, and subsurface. That included subsea and topsides optimization and cost engineering to right-size facilities for planned well delivery.

Led reserves and resource assessments

As Reservoir Engineering and Production Director at Kosmos, I ran the company's year-end PRMS and SEC reserves process and worked directly with banks on reserves. I've worked with third-party auditors DeGolyer and MacNaughton, Netherland Sewell, and Ryder Scott. At Hess, I led the reserves work for my assets, including technically validating Africa's reserves and resources and leading their self-certification for multiple years.

Built the Kosmos first-generation reserves system

I created and implemented the company's global reserves management system and wrote its Reserves and Resource Manual for PRMS and SEC reporting.

Managed a waterflood in Lea County, New Mexico

My first engineering job was reservoir engineering on the North Monument Grayburg / San Andres Unit: 170 producing wells making 3,600 bopd, with 100 injection wells putting away 10,000 bwpd. We used tracer surveys and production data analysis to optimize water injection and to pick each year's drilling locations, improving sweep and maximizing reserves.

Trading and AI

Six years of trading

I traded futures full-time with a rules-based process. To get there I studied stocks, options, and futures extensively, and spent thousands of hours on price action, reading the daily chart down to the three-minute chart and learning how news and narratives move markets.

The hardest part was psychology: my own, and understanding what it takes to become a good trader. I worked with trading-psychology coaches for 18 months and learned that consistency comes from following a process, not from being clever. I judge myself on how well I followed the process, not on any single result.

Position sizing became the center of how I think about risk. Size comes from a system's own numbers and the drawdown you can actually live with, worked out through simulation, never from a rule of thumb. It's the same discipline as committing capital to a development under uncertainty.

Applied AI

Over the past two years I taught myself to build working software with AI tools, and I can apply them to reserves reporting, production monitoring, and data analysis.

Much of what I built is psychological tooling for traders. Trader OS is a coaching platform where traders check their readiness before a session, debrief after each trade, and work on the beliefs behind their mistakes, guided by an AI coach built from a real coach's own material. Each morning it can deliver a short coaching session in that coach's voice.

I also built a multi-agent AI development team that builds and tests the software, and the knowledge pipeline that turns an expert's material into a coach anyone can ask. The same idea works for any company that wants its best engineers' knowledge available to everyone.
