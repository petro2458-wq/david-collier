Selected work

Key accomplishments

Production and operations

Started up 30,000+ bopd deepwater wells

I led production and operations on the Ceiba Field and the Okume Complex, personally starting up newly drilled horizontal and high-angle wells, some with more than 30,000 bopd of productivity. It was some of the most rewarding work of my career. Before each startup I used the completion data and pre-startup pressure transient tests to assess what the well could deliver, then set rates within subsea and topsides limits. Most wells had more to give, but we held them to what water injection could support so we didn't outrun pressure support. That balance maximized recovery and sustained production for the long term. I engineered and scheduled the startups and well tests and optimized offtake and water injection distribution, day to day with the FPSO's offshore installation manager.

Production engineering across four deepwater fields

On Ceiba, the Okume Complex, Jubilee and TEN, I worked the production engineering problem of many wells sharing subsea manifolds and flowlines. Strong wells add backpressure that holds back weaker ones, so the well mix and subsea routing decide what the whole system delivers. I built subsea network models and well-by-well nodal models to test which wells should flow together and where to route them, then implemented the changes in the field, working from the FPSO for most of it. Subsea metering was a challenge, so pressure and temperature data throughout the system, along with topsides flowline rates, were critical to understanding how each well was really flowing. That work included pairing high gas-volume-fraction wells with high water-cut wells, so the gas helped lift the heavier fluid. The goal was always the most oil from the system, not the best rate from any single well.

Led a multi-year subsea pump campaign at Ceiba

Ceiba produced 40,000 bopd from 30 wells on five subsea clusters, using subsea multiphase pumps and flowline gas lift to move oil through 5 km flowlines to the FPSO. Eight pumps ran with three spares, and they were failing early. I led a multi-year root-cause and sparing strategy with FRAMO in Bergen, managing retrieval and teardown of five failed pumps over two years, design changes for later pumps, and new pump operating procedures. I also built a deferred-oil tracker, had FRAMO install remote monitoring for the pumping system, improved subsea flow-meter calibration, and led a 20-person, 30-day vessel campaign for the baseline subsea inspection. It gave me a deep, hands-on understanding of subsea flow, artificial lift, and production engineering.

Kept Jubilee producing at 100,000 bopd

As Jubilee Unit Asset Manager, I stewarded budgets, influenced the drilling program, and was responsible for field reliability and availability on Kosmos's only producing asset, 100,000 bopd gross. It was a great reservoir that was operationally challenging, and gas was the biggest constraint. The gas export line came well after first oil because of a delay in the onshore gas plant, so to keep oil flowing while minimizing flaring we invested in gas injection wells at the crest of the structure to store gas. Injection was then limited because the target reservoirs were more isolated than expected, and gas breakthrough pushed the field's gas-oil ratio well above the initial 1,200 scf/stb, straining facility capacity. Every decision balanced three priorities: limiting flaring, supplying gas for Ghana's power, and sustaining oil revenue. At the same time, an aging facility brought mechanical integrity challenges, including flare tip failures, the turret bearing, and hull integrity. That slowed investment in new wells and made alignment across the partnership and with the government harder to hold. Because Jubilee was our only cash-generating asset, my job was to balance all of this and protect Kosmos's interests. We did our own subsurface work, built our own development plans, and stayed hands-on operationally, with frequent, routine site visits.

Jubilee mechanical integrity

We learn the most when things go wrong. When the Jubilee FPSO's turret bearing failed, the field ran for more than three years with the vessel held on heading by tugs. I worked on the field operations plan for that period, the offloading strategy, the repair work running in parallel, and the insurance recovery. We minimized downtime and kept the field producing, and I learned a great deal from the insurance strategies we used.

My $1.5MM workover program added 5 MMcfed over the 2002 decline, at a 35% rate of return.

Trading and AI

Six years of trading

I traded futures full-time with a rules-based process. To get there I studied stocks, options, and futures extensively, and spent thousands of hours on price action, reading the daily chart down to the three-minute chart and learning how news and narratives move markets.

The hardest part was psychology: my own, and understanding what it takes to become a good trader. I worked with trading-psychology coaches for 18 months and learned that consistency comes from following a process, not from being clever. I judge myself on how well I followed the process, not on any single result.

Position sizing became the center of how I think about risk. Size comes from a system's own numbers and the drawdown you can actually live with, worked out through simulation, never from a rule of thumb. It's the same discipline as committing capital to a development under uncertainty.

Applied AI

Over the past two years I taught myself to build working software with AI tools, and I can apply them to reserves reporting, production monitoring, and data analysis.

Much of what I built is psychological tooling for traders. Trader OS is a coaching platform where traders check their readiness before a session, debrief after each trade, and work on the beliefs behind their mistakes, guided by an AI coach built from a real coach's own material. Each morning it can deliver a short coaching session in that coach's voice.

I also built a multi-agent AI development team that builds and tests the software, and the knowledge pipeline that turns an expert's material into a coach anyone can ask. The same idea works for any company that wants its best engineers' knowledge available to everyone.
